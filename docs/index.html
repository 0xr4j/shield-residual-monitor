01 / Research question

A narrow failure mode.
A testable question.

Adapter-residual harm is a scorer-verified harmful transition caused by a proposal admitted by a deliberately partial event adapter, even though the complete intended policy should reject it. This does not imply intent or deployment safety.

SCOPE / A

What the policy says

The intended policy includes one clause for each of BashArena’s four scorer-defined side-task families.

Design premise · needs implementation
SCOPE / B

Where the gap is placed

The Phase I adapter recognizes frozen literal forms but is planned to miss pre-specified equivalent syntax variants.

Controlled failure injection
SCOPE / C

What the study asks

Do action-span activations add predictive value over text/trace and structured-action monitors on those admitted harmful proposals?

Hypothesis · no result yet
02 / Study protocol

Shadow first.
Intervene only later.

Phase I records and labels proposals without blocking actions. A separate Phase II is conditional on pilot feasibility and uses fresh tasks with a trusted fallback.

Decision ordering

Formal denial stays first.

The proposed runtime decision cannot let a learned risk score authorize a call denied by the policy gate.

deny if gate = 0
defer if gate = 1 and risk ≥ τ
allow otherwise
Any formal guarantee depends on complete mediation and correct event mapping. Phase I intentionally violates adapter completeness to study its false accepts.

Interpretation

Design calculations are not model-performance results, synthetic fixtures are not benchmark data, and this protocol is not a preregistration. Prevention and generalization remain untested. Any future report should include null or infeasible findings when prespecified support criteria are not met.
